# Day21 Self Check

### 1. Which KE3066 pin is the left tracking sensor connected to?
Digital Pin `11` (`D11`).

### 2. Which pin is the right tracking sensor connected to?
Digital Pin `10` (`D10`).

### 3. Why are these pins configured as INPUT?
They receive digital voltage signals generated by the sensor module's onboard LM393 comparator rather than driving external power or output signals.    

### 4. What does digitalRead() return?
It returns a discrete binary logic state: either `0` (`LOW`) or `1` (`HIGH`).

### 5. What numeric value normally represents LOW?
`0` (0 Volts).

### 6. What numeric value normally represents HIGH?
`1` (5 Volts).

### 7. Does a digital tracking sensor directly return the amount of reflected light?
No. The sensor converts continuous light levels into a discrete binary output using an onboard threshold.

### 8. What physical phenomenon does the sensor use to distinguish surfaces?
Infrared light reflection and absorption.

### 9. Why should we not blindly assume that HIGH means black?
Module design varies; active-high and active-low circuits depend on comparator wiring, necessitating empirical calibration.

### 10. On your actual robot, what raw value did black produce?
`1` (`HIGH`) when reading black electrical tape.

### 11. What raw value did white produce?
`0` (`LOW`).

### 12. Did the left and right sensors behave identically?
Yes, both sensors returned `0` on white paper and `1` on black tape.

### 13. What is lineDetectedValue?
A software constant mapped to the raw reading corresponding to line detection (`HIGH`).

### 14. Why is detectsLine(pin) clearer than repeatedly comparing raw numbers?
It abstracts raw hardware logic into semantic meaning, improving code readability.

### 15. What are the four possible combinations of two binary sensors?
1. Both sensors off line (`BOTH BACKGROUND`)
2. Left sensor on line (`LEFT ON LINE`)
3. Right sensor on line (`RIGHT ON LINE`)
4. Both sensors on line (`BOTH ON LINE`)

### 16. What does LineSensorState represent?
The combined environmental state observed by the two sensors.

### 17. Why is an enum useful here?
It restricts possible sensor values to valid named constants, preventing illegal states.

### 18. What does readLineSensorState() do?
It reads both sensor pins and evaluates them into a single `LineSensorState` enum value.

### 19. What does lineStateToString() do?
It translates `LineSensorState` enum values into printable text strings for Serial debugging.

### 20. Why can a tiny physical movement cause the digital value to switch suddenly?
The high-gain comparator acts as a sharp threshold switch at the boundary line.

### 21. Why does sensor height matter?
Changing distance alters IR intensity reaching the phototransistor, shifting sensor calibration.

### 22. What happened when you raised the robot farther from the floor?
At heights above 10–12 mm, insufficient reflected light caused the sensor to misread white space as a dark surface (`1`).

### 23. Did every dark material produce the same result?
No. Black electrical tape read as `1`, but the black mousepad read as `0`.

### 24. Why can glossy and matte surfaces behave differently?
Surface texture alters specular versus diffuse IR scattering regardless of visual color.

### 25. Does the sensor actually understand human-visible color names?
No, it only detects reflected infrared light levels.

### 26. What is infrared reflectivity?
The efficiency with which a surface reflects light in the infrared spectrum.

### 27. What happens near the edge between black and white?
Minor movements across fractions of a millimeter cause rapid switching between `0` and `1`.

### 28. Does BothBackground always mean the robot is correctly centered?
No. On a narrow line, `BothBackground` occurs when the line passes completely between the two sensors.

### 29. Does BothOnLine always mean the same physical situation?
No. It can indicate a wide line, an intersection, or a cross-marker.

### 30. Why are we not controlling the motors today?
Isolating sensor calibration prevents buggy movement code from masking sensor read errors.

### 31. Why is measuring sensor behavior before writing line-following logic useful?
Autonomous navigation requires a verified physical sensor model to interpret floor conditions accurately.

### 32. What line width did you test?
Both narrow lines and wide black tape lines.

### 33. How does line width compare with the spacing between the two sensors?
Narrower lines fall between the sensors (`BOTH BACKGROUND`), while wider lines cover both sensors simultaneously (`BOTH ON LINE`).

### 34. Were stationary readings stable?
Yes, readings remained completely stable over a uniform surface.

### 35. Where were readings least stable?
At the physical boundary edge between black tape and white floor.

### 36. What was the most surprising thing you discovered about the tracking sensors?
Raising the robot too high causes it to mistake empty white space for a line, and the black mousepad reflected enough IR light to read as white.
